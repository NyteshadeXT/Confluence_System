
## Equipment Potency
Dungeon equipment separates a magic item's Potency from its magical properties. Potency represents the item's fundamental enhancement and provides the numerical progression necessary for weapons and implements to remain effective against increasingly powerful Dungeon threats. An item's enchantment instead determines what unusual or supernatural abilities the item possesses.

A Dungeon magic item does not automatically possess Potency. A weapon may possess a magical enchantment without granting any enhancement bonus, and Potency may later be added or improved separately.

### Potency
A weapon or implement with Potency grants its Potency value as an item bonus to attack rolls and damage rolls made using that item.

For example:
**Potency +1**
- +1 item bonus to attack rolls.
- +1 item bonus to damage rolls.

Potency bonuses do not stack. Use only the highest applicable Potency bonus.

The expected progression is:

| Dungeon Tier | Expected Potency |
|---|---|
| Iron | +0 to +1 |
| Bronze | +2 to +3 |
| Silver | +4 to +5 |
| Gold and Beyond | +6 or greater, as appropriate |

These are progression expectations rather than automatic character benefits. Potency must still be obtained through Dungeon treasure, crafting, upgrading equipment, or another appropriate source.

### Potency and Enchantment

Potency and enchantment are separate properties.

A weapon might therefore be:

**Dungeon-Bound Battleaxe**
- Potency: None
- Enchantment: None

**Wyrmtooth Dagger**
- Potency: None
- Enchantment: Wyrmtooth

**+1 Wyrmtooth Dagger**
- Potency: +1
- Enchantment: Wyrmtooth

This allows a favored weapon to remain relevant through upgrading rather than requiring characters to continually replace equipment simply to maintain expected combat mathematics.

Potency does not determine an item's Rank, rarity, or magical identity by itself.
