Crafting transforms the treasures recovered from Dungeons into equipment, consumables, and magical items. Rather than relying solely on gold, craftsmen combine rare materials, magical energy, and specialized knowledge to create objects of lasting value.

# Crafting Resources
Crafting requires one or more of the following resources.

## Materials

Materials provide the physical structure of an item. Materials are primarily used to construct weapons, armor, tools, and other permanent equipment and are typically gained as loot dropped by defeated creatures and challenges or gathered within Dungeons.

## Creature Cores
![[creature-core.png|hmed right]]

> _"When a Dungeon creature falls, most of its magic returns to the Dungeon. On rare occasions, a fragment of that power refuses to fade."_

Creature Cores are condensed fragments of magical energy left behind by Dungeon creatures. Unlike Quintessence, which is raw and unshaped magical power, a Creature Core carries the imprint of a specific Attunement, preserving the magical nature that defined the creature during its existence. A Fire-attuned creature may leave behind a Fire Core, while a massive predator might produce a Hunt Core or Might Core. Each Core represents a stable fragment of the Dungeon's magic, refined around a single concept rather than a particular species.

Creature Cores are among the rarest treasures recovered from a Dungeon.

### Acquiring Creature Cores
Creature Cores are not guaranteed rewards. When a Dungeon creature is defeated, its body dissolves into motes of magical energy that are reclaimed by the Dungeon. Very rarely, a portion of that energy condenses into a stable Core before dispersing. More powerful creatures are significantly more likely to leave behind a Creature Core than lesser foes, and powerful Dungeon guardians almost always possess one.

### Attunements
Every Creature Core is aligned with a single Dungeon Attunement. The same type of Core may be recovered from many different creatures, provided they share the corresponding magical Attunement. Examples include:
- Fire Core
- Growth Core
- Hunt Core
- Spider Core
- Crystal Core
- Stone Core
- Death Core
- Venom Core
- Wing Core
- Sound Core

### Uses

#### Magical Crafting
Creature Cores provide the defining magical property of an enchanted item. While Quintessence supplies the magical energy required to create enchantments, a Creature Core determines the nature of that enchantment. A Fire Core might allow a weapon to burn with magical flame, while a Growth Core could enable armor to regenerate damage over time.

#### Advanced Equipment
The most powerful magical equipment often requires one or more Creature Cores in addition to large amounts of Quintessence. Rare Attunements are especially valuable, as they enable enchantments that cannot be replicated through ordinary means.

#### Magical Research
Scholars, artificers, and Essence researchers prize Creature Cores as stable samples of condensed magical concepts. By studying them, they seek to better understand the nature of Dungeons, Essences, and the forces that shape magical reality.

### Appearance
Creature Cores resemble smooth crystalline spheres or multifaceted gemstones roughly the size of a large marble. Each glows with the distinctive colors and visual effects of its Attunement. A Fire Core flickers with ember-red light, a Crystal Core refracts brilliant prisms, while a Hunt Core pulses with faint emerald energy that seems to shift like unseen movement through tall grass.

Even after centuries, a Creature Core never loses its magical resonance.

|Creature|Core Chance|
|---|---|
|Minion|—|
|Standard|5%|
|Elite|15%|
|Solo/Boss|100%|

### Rarity
Creature Cores are exceptionally rare. Most Dungeon expeditions recover none at all, making each discovery a memorable event. Because they are consumed during the creation of powerful magical equipment, demand for Creature Cores has always exceeded supply.

To experienced adventurers, recovering a Creature Core often marks the true success of a Dungeon expedition, for it represents not merely wealth, but the opportunity to create something truly extraordinary.

## Quintessence
![[quintessence.png|hmed right]]

> _"Essence is possibility given form. Quintessence is possibility made real."_

Quintessence is the purest magical substance found within a Dungeon. Unlike mana or stamina, which are drawn upon and replenished naturally, Quintessence represents condensed fragments of a Dungeon's creative power—the same primordial energy used to shape creatures, forge magical items, and weave supernatural effects into reality.

It appears as tiny motes or crystalline droplets of shimmering violet-gold light that slowly drift through the air before coalescing into small gemstones or liquid-like globules. When touched, Quintessence feels both weightless and impossibly dense, as though it contains far more than its physical form suggests.

Every Dungeon produces Quintessence, though only in limited quantities. It is among the most sought-after treasures recovered by adventurers, crafters, and scholars alike.

### Acquiring Quintessence
Quintessence is obtained almost exclusively from Dungeons. Common sources include:
- Defeated Dungeon creatures.
- Treasure caches.
- Dungeon rewards.
- Rare environmental formations where magical energy has condensed.

Outside of Dungeons, naturally occurring Quintessence is virtually unheard of.

### Uses

#### Crafting Catalyst
Quintessence serves as the magical catalyst required to create enchanted equipment. While mundane materials provide the physical structure of an item, Quintessence binds magical properties into the finished creation. Without it, even the finest craftsmanship cannot produce a true magical item.

#### Item Enhancement
Existing magical equipment can be strengthened by infusing additional Quintessence. As an item's power grows, increasingly larger quantities of Quintessence are required to stabilize the enchantments.

#### Magical Reforging
Quintessence can reshape existing enchantments, allowing magical properties to be altered, replaced, or transferred during the reforging process. This allows treasured equipment to evolve alongside its wielder rather than being discarded for stronger replacements.

### Dungeon Economy
Because every magical item and every advanced Essence eventually requires Quintessence, it has become the universal magical currency of adventurers. Guilds, crafters, and kingdoms maintain strategic reserves, while expeditions often judge the success of a Dungeon delve as much by the Quintessence recovered as by any individual treasure. Despite its value, Quintessence cannot simply be stockpiled indefinitely. Skilled artisans continually consume it to create new magical equipment, reinforce existing relics, and fuel advanced magical research, ensuring demand always exceeds supply.

### Appearance
Quintessence naturally glows with soft violet and golden light. In its raw form it resembles floating crystalline droplets or tiny stars suspended within glass. When Dungeon creatures are defeated, small motes of this same energy frequently separate from the dissolving body before condensing into recoverable Quintessence.

### Rarity
Although relatively common compared to Awakened Essences or Awakening Stones, Quintessence remains an invaluable resource. Most Dungeon expeditions recover only modest amounts, and powerful Dungeons often guard the largest concentrations behind their greatest challenges. For many adventurers, a single handful of Quintessence represents weeks of wealth—and the promise of future power.

## Gold
Gold represents mundane expenses incurred during crafting.  Things like forge fuel, workshop maintenance, replacement tools, alchemical reagents and smithing supplies all require gold.  As such, gold remains an important component of crafting even after characters begin exploring Dungeons.

# Arcane Patterns
An Arcane Pattern is a magical blueprint describing how to create a specific enchanted item. Knowing a Pattern is just as important as possessing the necessary materials. Flaming Weapon, Boots of Spider Climbing, and Cloak of Resistance are all examples of patterns. Patterns may be discovered as Dungeon treasure, purchased from skilled artisans, awarded for completing quests, or reverse-engineered from existing magical items. Once learned, a Pattern is permanently known and may be crafted whenever the required resources are available.

## Pattern Complexity
Every Pattern possesses a Complexity rating. Complexity represents the difficulty of successfully binding the Pattern into an item. Simple Patterns represent straightforward enchantments, while Complex Patterns often manipulate space, time, perception, or multiple magical effects simultaneously.

|Complexity|Modifier|
|---|--:|
|Simple|+0|
|Moderate|+2|
|Complex|+5|

# Crafting an Item
Crafting consists of four stages.

## Step 1 — Learn the Pattern
The crafter must possess the required Arcane Pattern and be at least Trained (as determined by the Arcane Pattern) in the crafting discipline required to create the item.  Without proper training and a Pattern, magical items cannot be crafted.

## Step 2 — Gather Resources
The required Materials, Ingredients, Creature Cores, Quintessence, and Gold must all be available before work begins.

## Step 3 — Accumulate Progress
Crafting requires time. Every recipe lists a required amount of **Crafting Progress**. Progress represents construction, experimentation, refinement, testing, polishing, and magical preparation. Progress accumulates while the crafter works during downtime. Progress is never lost. Characters may stop work at any time and resume later.

## Step 4 — Complete the Craft
Once sufficient Progress has been accumulated, the crafter makes a single Crafting Check. Success completes the item. Failure means the item requires additional work before another attempt can be made.

## Crafting Progress
Each recipe specifies the amount of Progress required before completion may be attempted. Characters accumulate progress while working on an item. The amount of Progress a character produces each hour depends upon:
- Crafting Discipline
- Crafting Rank
- Workshop Quality
- Tool Quality
- Assistants
- Special abilities

Progress is semi-permanent. Work may be paused at any time and resumed later without penalty. This allows characters to begin projects between expeditions and gradually complete them over multiple periods of downtime. For example, a suit of armor requiring 60 Progress may be worked on over several evenings until complete. Failure of any kind during the final Crafting check can reduce progress.

### Rushing or Refining
A crafter is not required to complete an item immediately upon reaching its required Progress. They may choose to finish early or continue refining their work.

## Crafting Check
When the crafter decides the item is complete, make a Crafting Check.

> d20 + Intelligence modifier + Crafting Discipline + bonuses

The Base Difficulty Class is determined by the item's Rank and the Pattern's Complexity.

|Rank|Base DC|
|---|--:|
|Iron|15|
|Bronze|20|
|Silver|25|
|Gold|30|
|Platinum|35|

### Craft Type
Beginning with an existing item is generally easier than creating one from raw materials. Modify the crafting DC as follows.

|Craft Type|Modifier|
|---|--:|
|Enhance an existing magical item|+0|
|Enchant an existing mundane item|+2|
|Craft entirely from raw materials|+5|

## Final Difficulty
> **Crafting DC = Rank DC + Pattern Complexity + Craft Type Modifier**

## Results
- **Critical Success** The item is crafted flawlessly. In addition to completing the item, you recover 1 Quintessence used during crafting and the Arcane Pattern's required Progress by is by 25% the next time this pattern us used to craft an item.
- **Success** The item is completed successfully.
- **Failure** The item is incomplete. No resources are lost. 25% of existing Progress is lost, but the crafter may continue accumulating additional Progress before attempting another Crafting Check.
- **Critical Failure** The magical infusion destabilizes. All Progress is lost and the crafter must rebuild the magical infusion before attempting the Crafting Check again. Quintessence and Ingredients are expended and lost.  Other materials like Creature Materials and Creature Cores survive a failed attempt. 

## Crafting Multiple Batches
A crafter may produce multiple batches of the same item as a single crafting project.

Multiply all required Supplies and Crafting Progress by the number of batches being produced. After determining the total required Progress, reduce it by 25% (round up) to represent the efficiencies gained through batch production.

The entire project is resolved with a single Crafting Check.

**Example:** A Potion of Healing requires 10 Progress. Crafting six batches would normally require 60 Progress. Applying the 25% reduction lowers the total required Progress to 45.

# Assistance
Multiple craftsmen may work together on the same project. One character serves as the Primary Crafter. Additional assistants contribute a portion of their normal Progress while assisting. Only the Primary Crafter must meet the recipe's minimum Crafting Rank but all assistants must be at least Trained in a crafting skill to provide proper support.

# Upgrading Equipment
Equipment is intended to evolve alongside its owner. Rather than replacing treasured equipment, crafters may improve existing items. Each upgrade requires additional materials, Creature Cores, Quintessence, and often a new Arcane Pattern. 

# # Upgrading Equipment
Equipment is intended to evolve alongside its owner. Rather than replacing treasured equipment, craftsmen may improve existing items as they gain access to better materials, more powerful Arcane Patterns, and greater quantities of Quintessence.

Upgrading an item follows the normal crafting process, requiring the appropriate Crafting Discipline, Progress, and a successful Crafting Check.

Typical upgrades include:

## Material Improvements
Replace portions of an item with superior materials while preserving its identity. Material upgrades improve the item's physical quality and may unlock future enhancements.

## Magical Enhancements
Add new magical properties to an existing item. Magical enhancements require the appropriate Arcane Pattern, Quintessence, and often one or more Creature Cores aligned with the desired enchantment.

## Rank Advancement
As a character advances, trusted equipment may be reforged to match the power of higher Dungeon Ranks. For example, an Iron Rank weapon may eventually be reforged into its Bronze Rank and Silver Rank equivalents without replacing the original item. Rank advancement typically requires superior materials, additional Quintessence, and significantly greater Crafting Progress.

# Recipe Discovery
Not every recipe is known from the beginning. Characters may discover new recipes through:
- Dungeon exploration
- Ancient libraries
- Forgotten workshops
- Guild instruction
- Master craftsmen
- Quest rewards
- Reverse engineering magical equipment

The pursuit of new recipes is intended to be a meaningful form of character progression alongside Essences, Awakening Stones, and Quintessence.

# Recipe Discovery
Arcane Patterns are not common knowledge. Most craftsmen begin their careers without even a simple Pattern, with more advanced techniques becoming increasingly difficult to acquire. Once a Pattern has been learned, it is permanently understood and may be crafted whenever the required resources are available.

Characters may discover new Patterns through many means, including:
- Dungeon exploration
- Ancient libraries
- Forgotten workshops
- Guild instruction
- Master craftsmen
- Quest rewards
- Reverse engineering magical equipment

Some Patterns exist as written formulas, while others are encoded within enchanted schematics, runic tablets, or forgotten artificer's journals. Particularly powerful Patterns may be unique discoveries, known only to a single Dungeon or long-lost civilization.

Learning a Pattern requires time spent studying and understanding its magical principles, but does not consume the Pattern itself unless noted otherwise.
