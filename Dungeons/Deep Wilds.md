# Location 1 - The Threshold Grove

**Encounter Difficulty:** None (Exploration)

![[deep-wilds-threshold-grove.png|hmed right]]

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The moment you step through the shimmering doorway there is a strange sensation, like passing through a thin sheet of water. The air then changes. 
> 
> Gone is the familiar Deep Wilds. In its place stands an impossibly ancient forest beneath a vaulted canopy hundreds of feet overhead. Shafts of emerald light pierce the leaves like cathedral windows, illuminating drifting spores and motes of crystal dust.
> 
> Before you stretches a broad glade carpeted in moss and flowering fungi. Towering trees ring the clearing so densely that only two obvious paths remain.
> 
> Behind you stands the stone archway through which you entered. It rises from the forest floor exactly as it did outside...except that no portal remains lies within it. Only a few faint swirling motes of emerald light remain. Beyond the arch lies only more forest.
> 
> Opposite the entrance, another ancient stone staircase climbs toward an identical archway. Beyond the opening of the archway you see more of the dense forest.
> 
> Somewhere deeper in the forest...
> 
> something answers your arrival.

## Description

This clearing marks the Dungeon's **Threshold**—the place where every expedition begins. The Dungeon intentionally presents two stairways.
- The southern staircase is the entrance the party used.
- The northern staircase appears to lead toward another exit but currently terminates over empty space.

This immediately establishes that the Dungeon has **multiple entrances**, but only one is presently connected to the outside world. The second arch cannot be reached by conventional means. It becomes important much later in the campaign when additional Doorways begin linking to the Dungeon.

The Dungeon is watching. The first time a player touches a tree, examines a crystal, gathers mushrooms or approaches the second staircase everyone hears distant movement. Yet, nothing appears.

### Xavious

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> As Xavious crosses the threshold, he feels a sudden pressure in his chest. 
> 
> The Shard grows warm beneath his skin. For an instant, everyone hears a faint metallic clicking—like dozens of tiny gears turning somewhere impossibly deep inside him. 
> 
> Xavious feels something moving beneath his flesh. That's when the pain begins. Searing, stabbing, tearing, debilitating pain. Xavious drops to his knees and his eyes roll into the back of his head. The strange lattice of silver and black metal embedded in his chest begins to unfold. Sections rotate. Tiny clockwork arms extend. Pieces that have remained motionless since the day of its discovery suddenly realign with deliberate precision. 
> 
> The device reshapes itself into an angular **V**, pointing downward toward his heart. Four empty recesses become visible. Three sit evenly spaced across the upper arms of the V. A fourth rests below them at its center. Each cavity is smooth... perfectly formed. 
> 
> First, the movement stops. The metal locks into place with a soft _click._ The pain slowly dims to a dull ache and then. Silence.

Give the players a minute or two to recover from the situation.  Let them think that whatever is happening to Xavious is over.

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> Then, for the first time... they hear a voice.
> 
> It sounds strange—almost layered—as though several distant voices are speaking the same words a heartbeat apart.
> 
> "...recognized..."
> 
> A pause.
> 
> "...First Threshold..."
> 
> Another pause.
> 
> "...Dungeon located."
> 
> Several moments pass before it speaks again.
>
> "...Designation... incomplete." "...Memory... fractured." "...Purpose...,  ...Purpose..., ...Purpose..."
> 
> Another long pause.
>
> "...Seek." Afterward the voice falls silent.

The Shard occasionally "remembers" something when the party encounters something significant.

For example:

 **Seeing the Door** "Threshold."
**After entering** "Interior... confirmed."
**Seeing Monster Cores** "Core." "...Required."
**Seeing the first Essence Crystal** A much longer pause. "...Essence." Almost as though it recognizes an old friend.
**Looking at a Dungeon Seed** Long silence. Then, almost reverently "...Heart." The voice hesitates. "...Do not..." Silence. It cannot finish the thought.
**Seeing a Stability change** "...Instability increasing."
**If monsters respawn** "...Reconstitution."

If the players ask questions Sometimes the Shard simply answers, "Unknown", "Memory unavailable" or "...Not yet" depending on the situation. The Shard should feel like a broken artifact that was built to interact with Dungeons before the Shattering. Every statement is less a conversation and more an involuntary recollection triggered by familiar stimuli. The players should quickly realize that it recognizes Dungeons, it recognizes Essences, it recognizes Seeds,  it knows terminology no one else knows **but it doesn't understand why anymore.**

## The Four Recesses
Initially, they're simply four empty sockets of unknown purpose. Players might speculate they're meant for gems, crystals, keys, or relics. Only much later, when Xavious obtains his first Essence, does one of the recesses fill with a tiny, luminous construct that matches the Essence's nature. At that moment, the Shard quietly says only one word, "...Awakening." 

## Features of the Area
**The Living Forest** The trees are impossibly large. Roots wider than houses disappear beneath the moss. None show signs of age or decay.
**Entrance Arch** Players attempting to pass back through the arch will find that it is currently dormant.  The archway is dormant. Only faint emerald motes drift lazily within its frame. Anyone stepping onto the platform feels a slight warmth, but nothing happens. After the party's first meaningful interaction with the Dungeon—such as surviving an encounter or experiencing Essence—the motes begin to respond to them. Upon returning, the runes awaken one by one and the portal reforms.
**The Silent Stair** An identical staircase rises to a second stone archway. Beyond the opening lies only more ancient forest. Though it appears complete in every way, the runes remain dark and no trace of portal energy stirs within it.
**Crystal Growths** Small crystal formations grow from exposed roots. These are harmless. Successful harvesting later teaches players that Dungeon resources regenerate over time.
**Dense Forest** Leaving the obvious trails is possible, but difficult. Moving through dense vegetation counts as difficult terrain. After roughly one hundred feet of forcing their way through the undergrowth, explorers inevitably emerge onto one of the Dungeon's established paths. They have not become lost—the terrain itself seems to guide them gently back toward the natural routes through the forest.

## Investigation
**Arcana DC 17:** The archway and the distant arch share the same magical signature. 
**Nature DC 17:** None of these plants belong anywhere in the Deep Wilds. They are healthy but ecologically impossible.
**Occultism DC 17:** The entire clearing feels like a liminal space—a threshold between worlds rather than a natural forest.
**Religion DC 17:** Nothing here feels divine. Whatever shaped this place is neither celestial nor infernal.
**Crafting DC 17:** The stone staircases were not carved by mortal tools.
**Perception DC 15:** Every trail leading away from the clearing seems subtly more traveled than the surrounding forest, as though generations of creatures instinctively avoid forging new paths.
**Survival DC 17:** No animal trails leave the clearing. Every path seems deliberately maintained despite no obvious signs that anyone—or anything—has created them.

## Development
As soon as the party commits to one of the two trails leading away from the clearing, read:

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> Somewhere far beyond sight, a deep resonant tone rolls through the forest like the call of an enormous unseen creature.
> 
> Birds take flight and the wind falls silent.
> 
> For just a heartbeat, every living thing seems to listen.
> 
> Then everything resumes as though nothing happened.

Unknown to the players, this is the Dungeon acknowledging that new Seekers have entered its domain.

> [!tip]+ Treasure
> None. The knowledge gained here is the party's first reward. They discover that the Dungeon is a living place governed by its own laws rather than those of the outside world.

# Location 2 -  The Root Nursery
**Encounter Difficulty:** Level 1 (20 XP)

![[deep-wilds-root-nursery.png|hmed right]]

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The narrow trail widens into a quiet grove ringed by enormous trees whose roots rise from the earth like the ribs of some ancient beast. Pale green crystals jut from bark and stone alike, casting soft emerald light across the moss-covered ground.
> 
> The clearing is unnaturally silent.
> 
> Then... the roots begin to move.
> 
> Clumps of moss fall away as several insect-like forms pull themselves free from the earth. Their chitin appears grown from polished bark and crystal rather than flesh, and faint purple-gold light glows beneath the seams of their bodies.
> 
> Without hesitation, they charge.

## Description
This grove is one of the Dungeon's first defensive manifestations. It is not a nest, nor does it contain living creatures in the traditional sense. The Dungeon simply shapes essence into familiar forms to repel intruders. The manifestations resemble juvenile ankhegs, but they are not animals. Their bodies are seamless constructions of wood, crystal, and hardened essence. 

At the start of the encounter, place the following foes on the map:
- 4: [[Ankheg Broodling]]

```encounter
name: The Root Nursery
party: Deep Wilds
creatures:
  - 4: Ankheg Broodling
```

## Tactics
The broodlings possess no instinct for self-preservation. They rush the nearest Seeker in an attempt to overwhelm isolated targets through numbers. They never retreat, surrender, or pursue fleeing characters beyond the boundaries of the clearing.

![[deep-wilds-treasure-orbs.png|hmed left]]

## Features of the Area
- **Lighting:** [[Vision and Light|Dim light]] provided by crystal growths throughout the grove.
- **Clearing:** Approximately 50 feet in diameter.
- **Massive Roots:** Provide cover and break lines of movement.
- **Crystal Clusters:** Count as difficult terrain.
- **Dense Undergrowth:** Beyond the established trail, thick vegetation counts as greater difficult terrain. The forest resists movement away from the path.

## Investigation
Characters who examine the area before or after combat may attempt the following:
- **Arcana DC 15:** The manifestations are constructed from concentrated magical essence rather than living tissue. **DC 17** The Dungeon appears capable of creating temporary physical forms from raw magical energy. 
- **Arcana** - Players attempting to use Arcana on the crystalline droplet will be unable to determine what it is.  Describe it as: "A violet gold crystalline droplet pulsing with magical energy."
- **Nature DC 15:** These creatures imitate ankhegs but possess no true biology. They are something entirely different.
- **Perception DC 14:** There are no tracks entering or leaving the grove beyond those made by the party.

> [!tip]+ Treasure  
> As the final manifestation dissolves, the last of the purple-and-gold wisps do not fade. Instead, they circle one another before condensing into a pair of softly glowing orb suspended just above the moss.  One is emerald in color and the other Violet. Touching the orb causes it to dissolve into harmless light, leaving behind:
> 
> - consumable::[[Potion of Healing]] [rank::iron] [gp::50] [qty::001]
> - consumable::[[The First Woodsmans Lessons]] [rank::iron] [gp::???]

# Location 3 – The Silent Hunt
**Encounter Difficulty:** Level 1 (20 XP)

![[deep-wilds-silent-hunt.png|hmed right]]

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The trail narrows as towering roots weave together overhead, forming a natural archway that blocks much of the emerald light. Moss muffles every footstep, and hanging vines sway gently despite the still air.
> 
> The path ahead appears empty. Then a branch snaps and you notice a flash of movement vanishing behind a tree.
> 
> Another follows from the opposite side.
> 
> Your first thought is, "the forest has begun to hunt you."

## Description
Unlike the Root Nursery, this clearing demonstrates that the Dungeon is capable of coordinated tactics.

The manifestations do not rush the Seekers immediately. Instead, they stalk from cover, waiting for an opportunity to strike before slipping back into the forest.

At the start of the encounter, place the following foes on the map:
- 2: [[Blood Hawk]]    

The Blood Hawks should begin hidden behind trees or perched in elevated positions if the map allows.

```encounter
name: The Silent Hunt
party: Deep Wilds
creatures:
  - 2: Blood Hawk
```

## Tactics
The blood hawks are patient. They focus on isolated targets rather than the closest enemy.

If both Blood Hawks can attack the same character, they do so before separating to opposite sides of the battlefield.

Although they appear to retreat into the trees between attacks, they never leave the encounter area.

Like all Dungeon manifestations, they fight until destroyed.

## Features of the Area
- **Lighting:** [[Vision and Light|Dim]].
- **Trail:** Roughly 15 feet wide.
- **Trees:** Massive trunks provide cover and block line of sight.
- **Low Branches:** Allow the Blood Hawks to perch before diving.
- **Dense Vegetation:** Counts as difficult terrain for creatures leaving the trail.
- **Crystal Growths:** Emit soft emerald light but do not illuminate the entire grove.

## Investigation
Characters examining the grove may attempt the following:
- **Nature DC 15:** These resemble hawks only superficially. Their movements are unnaturally synchronized.
- **Perception DC 16:** The "birds" never make a sound—not even wingbeats.

> [!tip]+ Treasure  
> As the second Blood Hawk dissolves into drifting purple-and-gold wisps, a handful of the lights spiral together before condensing into another lustrous violet orb. Touching the orb reveals:
> -  consumable::[[Treatise of the Hidebound]] [rank::iron] [gp::???]
> 
> A DC 16 perception check to investigate the area reveals the blood hawk nest.  In the nest the players find a strange rune.
> - consumable::[[Foundation Sigil]] [rank::iron] [gp::???] [qty::002]

# Location 4 – The Verdant Crossing
**Encounter Difficulty:** Level 1 Hazard (20 XP)

![[deep-wilds-verdant-crossing.mp4|hmed right]]

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The trail opens into a vast circular cavern where an immense pool of crystal-clear water stretches from wall to wall. Massive roots, each thicker than a wagon, weave slowly beneath the water's surface. Every few moments one rises just high enough to breach the surface before silently sinking beneath the water once more.
> 
> Pale green fungi blanket the exposed roots, releasing drifting clouds of luminous spores whenever they move. 
> 
> From the middle of the basin, the trail branches in several different directions beyond which lies either another stone archway or a plain stone door.
> 
> There is no bridge; only the moving roots.

## Description
The Verdant Crossing is the party's first encounter with an environmental challenge created entirely by the Dungeon.

The roots continually shift beneath the basin according to a repeating pattern, forming temporary bridges before slipping beneath the water once more. While the route is completely safe once understood, impatience and poor footing awaken the basin's defensive mechanisms.

The Dungeon is not attempting to kill the Seekers. It is testing whether they can observe, adapt, and work together.

Unlike previous encounters, there are no hostile manifestations. The room itself is the encounter.

## Running the Encounter
This encounter is divided into **three phases**.

### Phase One — Observation
Allow the players to freely investigate the room. If they simply watch the basin for one minute (or approximately three complete root cycles), they automatically recognize that the roots follow a perfectly repeating pattern. No check is required. Successful skill checks simply reveal additional information sooner.

The players should eventually conclude:
> "The roots aren't random."

### Phase Two — Crossing
The crossing consists of five major roots. Only three are stable during each movement cycle. Each round, the stable roots change.

Characters may either:
- Wait and observe.
- Move cautiously.
- Rush across.

Waiting is always safe. Moving without understanding the pattern is dangerous.

### Phase Three — Recovery
Falling into the basin is not failure. It simply costs resources. The party can recover and continue crossing. Eventually everyone reaches the opposite side.

## Tactics
Although there are no creatures present, the Dungeon continually reacts to careless movement. Whenever a character:
- Rushes.
- Attempts to jump multiple roots.
- Moves onto an unstable root.
- Critically fails an Athletics or Acrobatics check.

The basin responds immediately. The root rolls beneath their feet. The character falls into the water. Nearby fungal mats rupture. Living roots attempt to entangle them.

## Features of the Area
**Crystal Basin** Approximately sixty feet across. The water is remarkably clear, allowing characters to watch the roots moving beneath the surface.
**Living Roots** The roots continually shift through the basin. They are sturdy enough to support several creatures when stable. When unstable, they slowly rotate beneath anyone standing on them.
**Luminous Fungus** Soft green fungi coat many roots. Disturbing them releases clouds of glowing spores.
**Crystal Water** The water is only chest deep. Characters who fall are never in danger of drowning. However, movement becomes difficult as roots begin wrapping around them.
**Opposite Shore** The exits remains clearly visible throughout the encounter. Nothing blocks it except the basin itself.

## Investigation
Characters may attempt the following before crossing.

**Nature DC 15** The fungi release spores only when physically disturbed. Remaining calm minimizes their spread.
**Perception DC 15** The roots follow a repeating sequence rather than moving randomly.
**Arcana DC 16** The roots are animated by concentrated Dungeon Essence.
**Athletics DC 15** The roots are surprisingly sturdy while above the surface. Attempting to leap several at once appears considerably more dangerous.
**Insight DC 16** The room feels less like a trap than a lesson. It seems designed to punish impatience rather than exploration.

> [!callout|bg-c-red] Hazard Effects
> ### Unstable Root
> **Trigger** A creature moves onto an unstable root.
> **Effect** The root slowly rolls. The creature must succeed on a DC 15 Acrobatics save or fall into the basin.
>
> ### Entangling Roots
> A creature in the basin must succeed on a DC 15 Athletics check.
> - **Success** The creature suffers no negative effects.
> - **Failure** [[Immobilized]] until the end of its next turn.
> - **Critical Failure** [[Restrained]] instead.
>
> ### Spore Burst
> Whenever a creature falls into the basin: All creatures within 2 squares must succeed on a DC 15 Fortitude save.
> - **Success** The creature suffers no negative effects.
> - **Failure** Take minor poison damage. Become [[Slowed]] until the end of their next turn.
> - **Critical Failure** Also become [[Dazzled]] by glowing spores.

## Development
Once the first character reaches the opposite side, the roots slowly come to rest. The fungi cease releasing spores. For the first time since entering the basin everything becomes still.

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The final root settles quietly beneath the water. Purple-gold wisps of energy form in front of you.  They coalesce into a pair of crystalline droplets form from the energy and drop to the ground before you with a slight tink sound. Glowing spores around you drift lazily downward until they disappear beneath the surface. For just a moment, the basin appears perfectly calm...
> 
> ...as though satisfied.
>
> As the area calms, Xavious hears the Shard quietly whisper:
> "...Adaptation."

> [!tip]+ Treasure
> Careful examination of the basin after the crossing reveals a small crystal lodged between two submerged roots near the far shore. Any character who succeeded on both the **Nature** and **Perception** investigation checks automatically notice the crystal while crossing.
> - consumable::[[Lightning|Lightning Essence]] [gp::???]
> - consumable::[[Quintessence]] [rank::iron] [gp::???] [qty::002]
> 
> Players who stop to investigate or fill waterskins are rewarded with something that initially looks like ordinary water but is noticeably different.
> - consumable::[[Crystal Water]] [rank::iron] [gp::???]

# Location 5 – The Hunter's Snare
**Encounter Difficulty:** Level 2 (30 XP)

![[deep-wilds-hunters-snare.png|hmed right]]

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The trail narrows once again before opening into a broad clearing strewn with fallen logs and massive, moss-covered roots.
> 
> Crystal growths protrude from the earth like jagged emerald teeth, their light broken by thick clusters of towering mushrooms that rise nearly shoulder-high. Vines drape lazily between ancient trees, creating countless pockets of shadow despite the emerald glow.
> 
> The clearing appears empty.

When the players move into the clearing, read the following:
> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> A familiar cry echoes overhead as a pair of blood hawks begin to circle.

Following the first blood hawk attack, read the following:
> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> Before your eyes can follow it, the ground erupts beneath your feet as some ankheg broodlings join the fray.

## Description

The Hunter's Snare demonstrates that the Dungeon's manifestations work together as a single ecosystem.

The Blood Hawks attack first, encouraging the party to seek cover among the fallen logs and crystal formations. Moments later, Ankheg Broodlings burst from beneath the roots, forcing the Seekers out of cover and back into the open. 

Neither manifestation commands the other. Instead, both behave as though they are following the same instinct. 

The Dungeon is no longer testing simple survival. It is testing battlefield awareness.

At the start of the encounter place the following foes:
- 2: [[Blood Hawk]]
- 2: [[Ankheg Broodling]]

```encounter
name: The Hunter's Snare
party: Deep Wilds
creatures:
  - 2: Blood Hawk
  - 2: Ankheg Broodling
```

## Tactics
The manifestations fight with surprising coordination. The Blood Hawks begin the encounter hidden in the canopy and do not attack until at least one character advances into the clearing. Once the first Blood Hawk attacks, both Ankheg Broodlings erupt from beneath concealed roots near the center of the battlefield. The manifestations instinctively pressure characters into poor positions but never appear to communicate.

**Blood Hawks** The blood hawks focus isolated or lightly armored targets. They attack from opposite directions whenever possible using trees and crystal formations to break line of sight before circling for another pass. They never remain adjacent if they can avoid it.

**Ankheg Broodlings** The ankheg broodlings rush characters attempting to engage the Blood Hawks. They attempt to block movement through the center of the clearing. 

## Features of the Area
**Lighting:** [[Vision and Light|Dim light]] provided by crystal formations.
**Fallen Logs:** Count as difficult terrain and provide [[Concealment]].
**Crystal Outcroppings:** Provide [[Concealment]] and block line of sight.
**Towering Mushrooms:** Lightly obscure creatures behind them. Entering a mushroom patch counts as difficult terrain.
**Dense Roots:** Several roots rise several feet above the ground, creating natural choke points.
**Forest Edge:** Thick vegetation surrounds the clearing. Creatures attempting to leave the encounter area quickly find themselves redirected by dense undergrowth, eventually emerging back along the established trail.

## Investigation
Characters examining the clearing before combat may attempt the following:
**Perception DC 15:** Several patches of disturbed soil suggest something recently burrowed beneath the clearing.
**Nature DC 16:** No natural predators would willingly hunt together in this manner.
**Arcana DC 17:** The crystal formations subtly resonate with the same magical essence animating the manifestations.
**Survival DC 15:** There are remarkably few tracks despite obvious signs of repeated activity.

## Development
As the final manifestation dissolves into drifting purple-and-gold wisps, the clearing grows silent once more. 

Xavious hears the Shard speak again.
> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> "...Coordination."
> 
> A pause.
> 
> "...Adaptation."

> [!tip]+ Treasure
> As the final manifestation dissolves, the remaining purple-and-gold wisps gather into four softly glowing orbs. Two are emerald, one is a brilliant gold, while the fourth burns with a deep azure light. Touching the bronze orbs reveals:
> - consumable::[[Foundation Sigil]] [rank::iron] [gp::???] [qty::002]
> 
> The azure orb dissolves into:
> - consumable::[[Quintessence]] [rank::iron] [gp::???] [qty::001]
> 
> The gold orb contains:
> - crafting::[[Creature Core - Hunt]] [rank::iron] [gp::???] [qty::001]

# Location 6 – The Stillroot Sanctuary
**Encounter Difficulty:** None (Sanctuary)

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The trail descends between two immense roots and opens into a narrow grove sheltered beneath their intertwined mass. The constant sounds of the forest fade as you enter, replaced by the gentle trickle of water and the slow chiming of crystal in the still air.
> 
> Pale mushrooms form quiet rings across a carpet of deep moss. At the center of the grove, a shallow basin has grown naturally from the roots of an ancient tree. Clear water fills the basin, glowing faintly with violet-and-gold light.
> 
> For the first time since entering the Dungeon, you feel as if nothing watches from the shadows.
> 
> Nothing moves to attack.
> 
> This grove feels... safe.

## Description
The Stillroot Sanctuary is the party’s first encounter with a location created by the Dungeon specifically for rest and recovery.

The grove is not merely free of hostile manifestations. The Dungeon actively suppresses violence within its boundaries. Aggressive impulses feel strangely distant, drawn weapons become heavy in the hand, and hostile magical effects lose cohesion before they can fully manifest. The Sanctuary represents another lesson in the Dungeon’s design:
> Survival requires more than overcoming danger. It also requires knowing when to stop.

This Sanctuary is expendable. Once its restorative power has been used, the grove remains physically safe, but its magical benefits become dormant. The Dungeon may restore the Sanctuary during an intermission, significant reset, or other period of reconstitution.

## The Sanctuary
The first time a Seeker touches the water in the central basin, read:
> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The water brightens beneath your fingertips. Lines of violet-and-gold light spread through the basin like roots beneath clear earth. The glow passes into the surrounding moss, climbs the ancient trees, and slowly fills the entire grove. The exhaustion of the journey suddenly becomes impossible to ignore—but alongside it comes the unmistakable certainty that, here, you may finally rest.
> 
> Somewhere within Xavious’s chest, the Shard gives a quiet metallic click.
> 
> “...Sanctuary.” A pause follows. “...Finite.”

The Shard’s final word is the party’s first warning that the Sanctuary is a resource rather than a permanent refuge.

## Sanctuary Benefits
While the Sanctuary remains charged, characters resting within the grove gain the following benefits:
- Hostile Dungeon manifestations cannot enter the grove.
- Characters may safely take a short rest.
- Each character regains all expended encounter powers.
- Each character may spend Healing Surges normally.
- Each character regains 1 Mana or 1 Stamina.
- Characters may rebalance their Essences and change their readied Encounter powers.
- Expressive Awakening Stones may be exchanged if the character possesses another compatible Stone.

The Sanctuary does not restore:
- Expended Daily powers.
- Expended Quintessence.
- Burned Lesser Awakening Stones.
- Healing Surges.
- A character from the Strained state unless that character otherwise meets the requirements to end it.

Once the party completes a rest and accepts the Sanctuary’s benefits, its restorative energy is consumed.

## Consuming the Sanctuary
When the Sanctuary is expended, read:
> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> As the last of you rises, the glow within the basin begins to fade. Violet-and-gold light retreats from the moss, withdraws through the roots, and gathers briefly at the basin’s center. For a moment it resembles a tiny star reflected in still water.
> 
> Then it goes dark.
> 
> The grove remains quiet. The air remains calm. Yet the deep certainty of protection has vanished, leaving behind only an ordinary pool beneath ancient trees. The Sanctuary given all that it was to protect and provide rest.

Afterward, the area can still be used as a defensible resting place, but it provides no special recovery and no longer prevents manifestations from entering.

## Features of the Area
**Sheltering Roots:** Immense roots arch over the grove, forming a vaulted enclosure approximately thirty feet wide and sixty feet long.
**Central Basin:** A shallow root-grown basin filled with clear water. The water is harmless and refreshingly cool but loses its magical qualities when removed from the Sanctuary.
**Moss Beds:** Thick moss provides comfortable places to sit or sleep. Characters resting here suffer no discomfort from the Dungeon environment.
**Crystal Chimes:** Slender crystals hang from branches surrounding the grove. They ring softly whenever a creature crosses the Sanctuary’s boundary.
**Suppressed Violence:** While the Sanctuary is charged, attacks cannot be completed within the grove. Weapons can be drawn and powers initiated, but hostile actions falter before resolving.

## Investigation
**Arcana DC 15:** The grove is surrounded by a stable field of Dungeon Essence that suppresses hostile manifestations. **DC 17:** The field is not permanent. Its energy is being stored within the central basin and will be consumed when used.
**Nature DC 15:** The plants within the grove are unusually dormant. None compete for light, nutrients, or territory.
**Perception DC 15:** The crystal chimes respond only when something crosses the grove’s boundary. Movement within the Sanctuary does not disturb them.
**Insight DC 16:** The Sanctuary does not feel charitable. It feels practical—as though the Dungeon has calculated that exhausted Seekers cannot provide whatever it expects from them.
**Occultism DC 17:** The protection surrounding the grove recognizes the party individually. It does not merely repel creatures; it distinguishes between Seekers and manifestations.
**Crafting DC 17:** The basin, roots, crystals, and protective field function as parts of a single magical mechanism despite appearing entirely natural.

## Attempting to Exploit the Sanctuary
The Sanctuary resists attempts to dismantle or harvest its active components. Removing crystals, cutting roots, draining the basin, or otherwise damaging the grove immediately ends its restorative effect. The Dungeon permits the Sanctuary to be used. It does not permit it to be taken.

Its shape resembles the outer ring of a [[Foundation Sigil]], suggesting that the Sanctuary and Dungeon equipment rely upon the same underlying magical architecture.

# Location 7 – The Corpseflower Garden
**Encounter Difficulty:** Level 3 (40 XP)

![[deep-wilds-corpseflower-garden.png|right hmed]]

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> Beyond the shelter of the Sanctuary, the trail descends sharply between walls of tangled roots. The air grows warmer and damp, carrying an earthy sweetness that quickly becomes cloying.
> 
> The passage opens into an immense sunken grove.

Curtains of luminous fungus hang from dead branches, bathing the clearing in muted green and violet light. Across the grove lie the immense, half-buried forms of fallen beasts—antlered predators, long-bodied reptiles, and creatures whose shapes you do not recognize.
> 
> None of them show signs of rot. Instead, mushrooms bloom from their hides, crystal roots wind through their bones, and clouds of golden spores drift from their open mouths with every faint movement of the air.
>

>

## Description
The Corpseflower Garden is the party’s first direct experience with the Dungeon’s Fungus Attunement.

The bodies scattered throughout the grove are not true corpses. They are discarded forms previously created by the Dungeon—old manifestations whose remaining structure has been repurposed into a fungal ecosystem. Mycelial networks thread through the hollow bodies, animating them when the garden is threatened.

This encounter introduces an important principle:

> Nothing within the Dungeon is wasted.

When the Seekers approach the central flower or disturb one of the apparent corpses, the fungal network awakens. Several bodies rise as spore-filled husks while smaller fungal manifestations emerge from beneath the moss. The garden is not a burial ground. It is a recycling system.

## Encounter Structure
This is a two part encounter.

### Initial Manifestations
The first group consists a pair of Sporeback Frogs concealed among the apparent remains. At the start of the encounter place the following foes:
- 2: [[Sporeback Frog]]

### The Corpseflower

> [!note]+ Read Aloud

> [!danger]+ The Corpseflower

> - **Nature** to identify and destroy the active mycelium.
> - **Arcana** to disrupt the flow of Dungeon Essence.
> - **Athletics** to tear the corpse free from the fungal network.

> Roots beneath the moss contract and rearrange the garden. Choose two fungal manifestations. Each may shift up to 2 squares without provoking opportunity attacks. Mushroom patches adjacent to either creature become difficult terrain until the end of the next round.

> ## Ending the Encounter

> **Destroy the Bloom** The flower can be attacked after opening.
> **Overload the Network** A character adjacent to the bloom may channel magic into it with a DC 18 Arcana check and expend 1 Mana. On a success, the network becomes unstable. The flower immediately triggers Spore Pulse against all remaining fungal manifestations, then collapses. On a failure, the character becomes subject to Spore Pulse instead.

> - **Athletics DC 16** to tear it free.
> - **Nature DC 15** to identify and cut the correct section.
> - A weapon attack against AC 10.

# Location 10 – The Living Grove

![[deep-wilds-living-grove.png|right hmed]]

## Description
### The Three Monoliths
Each represents one of the Dungeon's Attunements. The symbols are carved into each crystal, but the players won't necessarily recognize them immediately.
- Growth
- Fungus
- Hunt

Touching a monolith causes it to flare with light.

**Nature (DC 15)** The oldest trees grow around the Growth crystal. The fungal colonies flourish only where the roots have already spread. Animal tracks are concentrated around the fungal patches. **Clue** First comes the forest. Then decay nourishes it. Finally come the hunters.
**Perception (DC 15)** The roots themselves reveal a sequence. Closest to the Growth crystal are fresh roots.  Near Fungus you find moss, mushrooms and decomposition. Around Hunt are claw marks, feathers and bones. The life cycle is literally written into the grove.
**Dungeoneering (DC 15)** Dungeon manifestations don't appear randomly. Attunements shape one another. Growth creates life. Fungus recycles it. Hunt controls its abundance.
**Arcana (DC 16)** The mana flow circles the room. It clearly moves Growth → Fungus → Hunt before returning to Growth.

#### Activating a Crystal
Each crystal sends magical energy through the roots. Correct activation causes one-third of the roots sealing the exits to withdraw. Players immediately know they're making progress.

#### Wrong Order 
Whenever the wrong crystal is activated roots erupt from the ground. Every creature makes an Athletics or Acrobatics check. Any creature who fails the check is wrapped in roots which squeeze them for one round causing `dice: 1d4|form` damage. Should they get it wrong a second time **Fungus** is activated. harmless-looking spores fill the grove like a fog. Creatures take a -2 Perception penalty for five minutes. A third mistake activates **Hunt**. The forest suddenly goes silent. Dozens of unseen eyes watch from beyond the trees. Nothing attacks. It's psychological. The Dungeon is warning them.

# Location 11 – The Strangling Garden

**Encounter Difficulty:** Level 4 (80 XP)

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The trail descends beneath a curtain of hanging vines into a low, humid grove. Here, the forest floor disappears beneath enormous overlapping leaves, some broad enough to serve as tables. Pale flowers hang from vines overhead, their petals opening and closing in slow rhythm.
> 
> Near the center of the grove, several thick roots have broken through the earth. They twist around one another in a tangled mound beneath a cluster of brilliant emerald crystals.
> 
> Something moves beneath the leaves.
> 
> Not through them.
> 
> **Beneath them.**
> 
> A root rises from the soil like a serpent tasting the air.
> 
> Then another.
> 
> All across the clearing, the garden begins to move.

## Description

The Strangling Garden is an aggressively overgrown pocket of the Deep Wilds where the **Growth Attunement has become dominant**.

The plants themselves are real Dungeon flora rather than manifestations, but their growth has been accelerated to an unnatural degree. The creatures dwelling among them exploit that environment rather than merely occupying it.

For this encounter, I'd use **creatures appropriate to the Growth theme rather than reuse Blood Hawks, Ankhegs, or Needlefangs**. I don't want to lock those in until we choose from your available 4e creatures, because the encounter's creature selection should determine the exact Level 4 XP budget.

The important part is that the creatures are **not the entire encounter**.

The garden is changing around them.

## The Growing Battlefield

At the end of each round, the Dungeon causes one previously clear patch of the battlefield to erupt with vegetation.

Mark a **2×2 area** that isn't currently occupied by another Overgrowth patch.

That area becomes **Overgrowth** for the remainder of the encounter.

> [!callout|bg-c-green]+ Overgrowth  
> **Terrain:** Difficult terrain.
> 
> A creature that enters an Overgrowth square must spend 1 additional square of movement as vines catch at its legs.
> 
> Overgrowth does not block line of sight.

I would **not** require a saving throw every round or have the plants constantly attack. That's too much bookkeeping.

The danger is much simpler:

**the battlefield is getting smaller.**

Round 1, it's barely noticeable.

Round 3, movement is becoming awkward.

Round 5, the characters are fighting through a garden that has nearly swallowed the clearing.

That feels very Growth.

## Cutting Back the Growth

The important part is that the players can interact with it.

A character adjacent to an Overgrowth patch can use a **standard action** to clear one 2×2 patch using an appropriate weapon or power.

A character may instead attempt:

**Nature DC 15:** Identify the central vine feeding a patch. On success, clear the patch as a minor action.

**Arcana DC 16:** Disrupt the Dungeon Essence accelerating the plants. On success, suppress one patch until the end of the encounter.

Fire or other obviously destructive area effects could also clear vegetation at your discretion.

That creates a real decision:

> _Do I spend my action attacking the monster, or do I keep the battlefield under control?_

That's much more interesting to me than the vegetation simply inflicting damage.

## Tactics

Whatever creatures we choose should **benefit indirectly from the changing battlefield rather than be immune to everything**.

I'd look for creatures that:

- Have good mobility.
    
- Pull, push, slide, or knock prone.
    
- Attack at short range.
    
- Can exploit characters separated by difficult terrain.
    

The manifestations know exactly where the new growth will appear. They deliberately pressure characters toward areas that are about to become difficult to traverse.

They do **not**, however, ignore the Overgrowth. The plants are part of the Dungeon environment, not conveniently selective terrain.

That distinction matters because it makes the encounter feel ecological rather than gamey.

## Features of the Area

**Emerald Crystals:** Several large Growth-aspected crystal formations protrude from the center of the grove. They provide cover but do not block line of sight.

**Giant Leaves:** Broad leaves stand three to five feet high. Squares containing them provide concealment until they become Overgrowth.

**Hanging Flowers:** The enormous pale blossoms are harmless. Their petals slowly turn toward nearby movement.

**Root Mound:** The central tangle of roots is difficult terrain and provides cover. It cannot become Overgrowth.

**Dense Forest:** As elsewhere in the Dungeon, forcing a path beyond the encounter boundaries eventually redirects the character toward an established trail. The earlier rooms establish this behavior for the Deep Wilds.

## Investigation

**Nature DC 15:** The vegetation isn't simply growing quickly. Individual plants are completing weeks of growth in seconds.

**Arcana DC 16:** Magical energy flows from the central crystal formation into the surrounding root network. The crystals aren't creating the plants; they're accelerating what is already alive.

**Perception DC 15:** Several patches of leaves shift independently of the wind. Something is concealed beneath them.

**Dungeoneering DC 16:** The growth isn't random. New vegetation consistently erupts near movement and disturbances in the clearing. The Dungeon appears to be responding to the battle itself.

A character succeeding on the Dungeoneering check realizes **before combat begins** that remaining stationary will probably allow the vegetation to close around them.

## Development

Once the final manifestation is destroyed, don't have the plants immediately disappear.

Instead:

> [!note]+ Read Aloud  
> The last manifestation dissolves into twisting strands of purple and gold.
> 
> The vines continue to grow.
> 
> For several seconds they crawl across roots, stones, and fallen leaves, swallowing the battlefield beneath a thick carpet of green.
> 
> Then every plant stops at once.
> 
> New leaves unfurl. Pale flowers bloom across the clearing. What moments ago was a strangling mass of vegetation has become a strangely beautiful garden.
> 
> Among the flowers, several motes of purple-gold light begin to gather.

That reinforces an important distinction: **Growth isn't hostile. Uncontrolled Growth is hostile.**

## Treasure

I'd make this another place where **investigation produces something in addition to the normal Dungeon Orbs**.

The central crystal formation has caused a rare plant to mature during the encounter. After combat, a successful **Nature DC 15** check identifies it as harvestable without damaging it.

That gives us an opportunity to introduce **another crafting ingredient**, rather than every room's reward being an Essence/manual/equipment orb.

Then the Dungeon itself can still produce 2–3 orbs appropriate to where we are in the loot progression.

---

I particularly like this for **11** because it changes the nature of the battlefield without adding another complicated hazard stat block. The GM's entire environmental procedure is basically:

> **End of round → add one 2×2 patch.**

That's easy to remember at the table, visually obvious with terrain markers, and it makes **Growth** feel fundamentally different from the Fungus and Hunt expressions they've already encountered.

The next thing I'd settle is the **actual Level 4 creature package**. I'd prefer to choose that from the 4e monsters you already have available rather than inventing manifestations that don't correspond to your source material.
